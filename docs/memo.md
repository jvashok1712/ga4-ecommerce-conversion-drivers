# Memo: Getting new users to their Aha moment

**To:** VP Product · **From:** Ashok · **Re:** What makes new users buy, and what to test next

## The answer

Viewing a product in the first visit is the Aha moment. New users who view at least one product convert at **6.55%** within 30 days; those who don't convert at **0.31%**. Yet **only 1 in 5 new users views a product** in their first visit.

## Why we believe it

- **It survives controls.** Comparing users with the same engagement, device and traffic source, viewing a product still **doubles the odds** of buying (odds ratio 2.16).
- **It holds everywhere.** Viewers convert 17x–36x more on every device, traffic source and country.
- **It's where the funnel leaks.** 77.8% of new users never reach a product page, the single biggest drop.
- **Timing confirms it.** 67.6% of buyers buy on the day of their first visit. The first session decides the outcome.
- **It's predictive.** A simple model using only first-session behaviour identifies December buyers with AUC 0.94, trained only on November.

Not every engagement signal helps: site search (OR 0.66) and promo clicks (OR 0.60) are *negative* once engagement is controlled. We should not invest there.

## What it's worth

If 5 more in every 100 new users viewed a product (20% → 25%), we estimate **15–261 extra buyers** and **$1.3K–$22.3K extra revenue per month**. The wide range is honest: today's viewers are partly self-selected keen shoppers. Only a test can narrow it.

## Recommendation

**Run a 2-week A/B test** of a homepage product-recommendation row for new users.

- **Primary metric:** first-session product-view rate (baseline 20.2%). It reads out in days; testing on purchases directly would take ~75 days.
- **Guardrails:** 30-day conversion and revenue per user must not fall.
- **Sample:** ~12,852 users (≈ 4.7 days of traffic); run 2 weeks to cover weekly cycles.
- **Follow-up:** 17.6% of buyers buy on days 1–7, so a first-week reminder email is the next lever.

## Caveats

Correlation until the test proves cause. Public dataset is obfuscated by Google and covers the holiday season for one store.
